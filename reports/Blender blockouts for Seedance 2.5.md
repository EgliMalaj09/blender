# Blender Blockouts Put Seedance 2.5 on Rails

A Blender grey-box clip is the most reliable way to give Seedance 2.5 a specific camera move, shot timing and layout, because the model treats it as a native "motion and camera reference" that can carry **blockout timing**, and the vendor-style guide includes prompts built for exactly this job ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). Text camera language works only as "a tendency rather than a specification" and gives a different angle on each run ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)). A blockout turns the move into data. The playbook is short. Render a clean Workbench pass from the scene camera at the delivery aspect ratio, 24 fps, about 720p, trimmed to the shot. Strip every overlay. Write a four-part prompt (asset map, one-sentence brief, whole-second timeline, global constraints) that says what to inherit from the clip and what not to copy. Draft at 480p and finish at 720p or 1080p. The @viznfr note "Claude + Blender → Genjutsu" is a sound workflow with accurate credit figures. It oversells fidelity ("frame for frame"), its Blender details are out of date, and it is tied to a Higgsfield wrapper you don't need: every step maps onto Seedance 2.5 reference-to-video at the same or lower cost. Expect late-clip drift, stiff motion copied from mannequins, occasional left/right mirroring and identity drift on longer clips. A first test costs about **$20–50 in API spend**, or roughly 550 credits of a $49 / 1,000-credit Higgsfield plan, before re-rolls.

One caveat applies throughout. The most detailed "official" guide used here is hosted on docs.seedance.tv, a third-party site whose example videos are labelled "seedance-2-5-official-guide". It probably mirrors ByteDance's guide, but no ByteDance-hosted page could be reached to confirm that. Most platform specs and prices also come from search-result summaries rather than full page reads.

## Seedance reads a blockout as a reference, not an edit master

The most useful thing to know is how Seedance 2.5 sorts tasks. **Locked** tasks (editing, first/last frame, extension) let the input dictate the output's timeline or aspect ratio. **Unlocked** tasks (ordinary references, storyboards, keyframes, blockouts) treat inputs as constraints, and "output duration and aspect ratio remain independent" ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). A Blender clip uploaded as Video 1 is therefore a generation reference. You must set duration and aspect ratio yourself to match it, because nothing forces them to match. Genjutsu, by contrast, reportedly follows the source clip's length and ratio ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/higgsfield-genjutsu-alternative)).

The guide describes two ways of using a blockout. A **coarse blockout** carries shot order, camera positions, framing changes, subject paths and timing, while appearance comes from images. Its example reads "Use Video 1 only for the complete shot order, camera positions, framing changes, subject paths, and timing. Do not copy the blockout materials or character appearance." A **fine blockout re-render** already has complete structure and asks the model to "Fully re-render Video 1 while preserving its action paths, camera movement, timing, and framing exactly", and adds "Do not generate motion paths, axes, or camera guides" ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). A grey-box team is almost always doing a coarse blockout, so the prompt has to map every primitive to a final subject. fal's template is "<Blockout Subject A> in @Video 1 corresponds to <Subject A>" ([fal](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5)).

The input limits are generous. A request can carry up to **50 assets**: 30 images (each ≤4K), 10 video clips totalling 30 s, and 10 audio clips totalling 30 s. Output runs up to 30 s ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). fal adds per-clip limits of 1.8–30.2 s and ≤200 MB, MP4 or MOV ([fal API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api)). Resellers also list 24–60 fps input and a minimum of about **409,600 pixels**, so 854×480 just passes and 640×360 fails ([hiapi.ai](https://www.hiapi.ai/en/models/seedance-2.5-reference-to-video)). Output is a constant 24 fps per a third-party test, and no API exposes an fps parameter ([flixly](https://www.flixly.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-explained-2026)). The guide calls 1–8 subjects "most reliable", which is a useful cap on how many proxies you ask the model to track.

How faithful is the result? The best-documented independent run is Curious Refuge's. With a Claude-built Blender animatic as the video reference, three image references, 10 s, 16:9 and a 480p draft, "Seedance followed our Blender animation closely, giving us a good amount of control over camera angles, cuts, character positions" ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)). Close adherence is real. Nobody has published a quantitative fidelity measurement for any path.

## Build a boring reference: grey, clean, short and to scale

### Render a Workbench clay pass with nothing drawn on top

Sources agree on **Workbench** and a plain sky. Blender's own docs describe Workbench as ignoring scene lights and offering Flat, Studio or MatCap lighting ([Blender Manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/render/workbench/lighting.html)). CozyClay calls "a flat clay pass with a plain sky" the cleanest reference Seedance can get ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)), and Dreamina ships a "Clay Renderer" Blender plugin, which shows the vendor endorses the look ([Dreamina](https://dreamina.capcut.com/seedance/seedance-2-5-blender-model-workflow)). Our recommendation, which is our own reading and not a sourced finding, is **Studio lighting with cavity and shadows on and a light-grey single colour**. Workbench's Flat mode removes all shading, so the model loses the volume and parallax cues it needs to read the camera move. Use EEVEE only when the blockout must carry a lighting change, since the guide says blockouts can define "lighting rhythm".

Leakage is the cardinal sin. The guide says to "remove motion paths, axes, and camera frustums so they do not leak into the result" ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). CozyClay widens the list: "Outlines, gizmos, the ground grid, a selection highlight, a frame counter: any of these can come back in the generation as an object" ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)). Curious Refuge warns against small text in any reference ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)). A true render from the scene camera (F12 or `-a`) avoids viewport overlays. Beyond that, turn off the metadata stamp, keep object outlines off, and hide helper curves and track targets from render.

### Grey versus colour-coding is a real trade-off, so choose per shot

This is the one live disagreement on the Blender side. CozyClay says "Grey or flat clay. Colour is a cue the model preserves… A single highlight colour on the subject is the most you should add" ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)). Scenario's blockout skill builds "color-coded proxies at real-world scale" ([scenario-labs/skills PR #243](https://github.com/scenario-labs/skills/pull/243)). The open-source seedance-reskin tool uses flat, saturated, distinct colours per object to tell subjects apart and warns that a near-final-looking blockout gets its materials copied ([seedance-reskin](https://github.com/DisturbedMediaGroup/seedance-reskin)). Both camps describe the same mechanism: the model keeps the colour it sees. That makes colour a lever. Use **grey plus at most one highlight** for single-hero shots where you want the model to invent surfaces. Use **desaturated, well-separated hues** only when two or more similar subjects must stay distinct. Either way, decode every colour in the asset map and add the guide's "Do not copy the blockout materials". Nobody has run a controlled A/B, which is why the test plan below includes one.

We also suggest a 6–12 frame hold or ease-in at the start so the model reads the opening composition before the move. That is cinematography practice, not a tested Seedance behaviour.

### Real scale, real lenses, eased moves and soft stand-ins

CozyClay's recipe is "Boxes and a stand-in figure, at real scale", then "camera height, distance, lens, then the move: a dolly rail, a crane, a hold" ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)). Focal length, path and timing "transfer directly to the model as a motion reference" ([flick.art](https://flick.art/blog/blender-ai-filmmaking)). Script moves with Bezier-eased keyframes, a follow-path or parented dolly, and a Track To aim target. Avoid linear snaps and unintended roll. Character stand-ins are where blockouts hurt most. Scenario found that "jointed figures with rigid, stepping legs get copied stiffly by Seedance": five runners came back "upright, in near lockstep" ([scenario-labs/skills #275](https://github.com/scenario-labs/skills/issues/275)). Curious Refuge saw Seedance follow the reference "almost too closely, including its unnatural character movements" ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)). Default to **legless capsules** for people. Model hero products accurately, because Scenario reports that accurately modelled props "come back exact". For crowds, keep geometry as instances, which the Blender docs note render cheaply without realizing ([Blender Manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/modeling/geometry_nodes/instances.html)). Describe the crowd as a texture in text instead of mapping hundreds of proxies.

### Let Claude Code script it headless, and target Blender 5.x

There are two Claude routes. **Claude Code plus a `build.py` run with `blender -b -P build.py`** is reproducible, diff-able, batchable and needs no GUI. The official **Blender Lab MCP connector** for Claude Desktop is live and interactive, but it is experimental, requires Blender 5.1+, and Blender warns it "will execute LLM generated code in Blender without any guards" ([mcp.film](https://mcp.film/mcps/blender-lab-mcp/); [Claude tutorial](https://claude.com/resources/tutorials/using-the-blender-connector-in-claude)). Curious Refuge found Claude good at layout but noted it "didn't add the camera movements we'd hoped to see" ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)). So specify camera moves numerically (metres, millimetres, frame ranges, interpolation) rather than leaving them to the model. Two Blender 5.x API changes break older scripts. You must set `image_settings.media_type = 'VIDEO'` before `file_format = 'FFMPEG'` ([Blender #152175](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/152175)), and EEVEE's identifier is `BLENDER_EEVEE` again ([Blender 5.0 notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/eevee/)). Blender 5.3 is current and 5.2 is the LTS ([Phoronix](https://phoronix.com/news/Blender-5.3-Released); [endoflife.date](https://endoflife.date/blender)). Ask Claude to render a few PNG stills first, check framing on them, and verify the MP4 with `ffprobe`.

## Prompt for what the clip cannot carry

### Use the four-part structure and bind every asset

The guide's structure is "**Asset mapping + one-sentence brief + timeline or shot order + global constraints**". Each asset must answer "Who or what does it belong to? What specifically should be referenced?" and, where needed, what must *not* be inherited ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). On API hosts, tags are positional and 1-indexed: **@Video1, @Image1…** follow the order of `video_urls` and `image_urls`, not filenames ([fal API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api); [Runware](https://runware.ai/docs/models/bytedance-seedance-2-5/guides/multi-reference-production)). The guide itself uses plain "Video 1 / Image 2" prose. No source documents a bracket syntax such as `[Video1]`. Set aspect ratio, duration and resolution as UI or API parameters. fal's `aspect_ratio` accepts auto, 21:9, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4 and 9:16 ([fal](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/what-is-seedance-2-5)). Pick an explicit duration rather than -1/auto, which in one test defaulted to 10 s and doubled the cost ([Segmind Extend](https://www.segmind.com/models/seedance-2.5-extend)).

### Restate the timing, not the camera

Sources split on whether to describe the camera in text. CozyClay says "Do not describe the camera again in words; the clip already did" ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)). The editing guide likewise prefers "preserve the original action order and timing exactly" over re-describing the action ([docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)). Curious Refuge found that repeating shot timings "can help Seedance follow the sequence" ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)). These positions fit together. Write a timeline that matches the blockout's cut points and says *what happens* in each range (subject, action, emotion, sound), and leave out *how the camera moves*. If you do mention a move, it must match the clip exactly, because a text-clip mismatch is the conflict the guides warn about. Use whole-second, continuous ranges: "Seedance 2.5 can follow whole-second timestamps… without unexplained gaps". Match density to duration, because "if a time range contains too little, the model will improvise; if it contains too much, actions may be omitted" ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)).

### Keep global rules static and name the placeholders

Global constraints are rules that "apply throughout", such as camera position, lighting, colour, ambience, no subtitles and no BGM ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). Put no timed event there. An event without a timestamp can appear from frame 0. For the same reason, phrase reveals as transitions at a time ("at 6 s the door opens and the bottle becomes visible") rather than states ("the bottle is already on the table"). This follows from the guide's rule that triggers be "timed explicitly". It has not been tested as a rule of its own. Two negative lines do most of the anti-leak work: the guide's "Do not copy the blockout materials or character appearance… Do not generate motion paths, axes, or camera guides", and Curious Refuge's "Treat the 3D figures as placeholders rather than copying their exact poses or body mechanics." No one has measured whether the placeholder line costs blocking accuracy, so test it.

## Pick the host on price, and set parameters yourself

Every host checked offers reference-to-video with video inputs: BytePlus ModelArk, fal, the Higgsfield API, Replicate and Atlas Cloud, plus the Magnific, Higgsfield and Dreamina apps. On ModelArk-style token billing (also used by fal and the Higgsfield API), tokens ≈ (input video seconds + output seconds) × width × height × 24 / 1024, and the rate drops to 0.6× when a video input is present ([Higgsfield API playground](https://open.higgsfield.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/playground)). **The blockout's own seconds are billed**, which is another reason to trim it. Sources disagree on the minimum billable input ([sjolt](https://sjolt.ai/pricing/seedance-2-5-video-input)). ModelArk charges only for successful generations ([cellcog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-pricing/)).

The 12 s figures are our own arithmetic from the per-token rates. Resolution claims are the most contested spec. fal's schema lists 480p, 720p and 1080p ([fal API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api)), OpenRouter shows a 720p cap ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/seedance-2-5-review/)), and 4K is reportedly enterprise-only ([orcarouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-explained)). Plan around 1080p as the ceiling. For iteration, **draft-then-finalize beats re-rolling at high resolution**. Segmind measured a 480p draft and its 1080p finalize as the same video (correlation 0.96), while a direct 1080p render with the same seed correlated only 0.257 with the draft ([Segmind](https://blog.segmind.com/seedance-2-5-draft-final-5-clips-tested-and-the-real-cost-math/)). Draft mode is confirmed on fal's image-to-video endpoint but not on reference-to-video ([fal I2V](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/image-to-video/api)), so verify it during the test.

The face filter matters most for platform choice. ModelArk docs, as quoted secondhand, say Seedance 2.5 does "not support direct uploads of reference images or videos containing real human faces", and the filter reportedly flags realistic renders and the model's own outputs too ([seedrouter](https://seedrouter.ai/blog/seedance-real-person-error); [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-404/seedance-2-5-firefly-is-having-issues-with-ai-generated-faces-it-categorises-ai-generated-faces-as-real-human-face-and-blocks-the-generation-1643856)). Grey capsules pass easily. Photoreal character sheets may not. Don't try to evade the filter: BytePlus policy bars it. The sanctioned route is a verified real-person asset library ([APIYI](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/faq/seedance2-asset-face-reference)). Our recommendation is **fal for the first test**: it has the clearest documentation, positional tags and explicit aspect, duration and resolution fields, and it costs about $3–7 per 12 s. Move bulk drafting to **ModelArk** once the prompt is stable, at roughly half the cost. A team that prefers a UI can use Higgsfield's Seedance 2.5 page, which runs at the same per-second credit rate as Genjutsu.

## Plan multi-shot work around drift, not around cuts

Whether Seedance can hold cuts inside one generation is disputed. CozyClay says "two references, two generations… models do not hold a cut inside one generation reliably" ([CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)). The official guide demonstrates a 30 s multi-stage blockout that says "Preserve Video 1's shot structure exactly, add no new shots" ([docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)). Curious Refuge got a working establishing/medium/over-the-shoulder sequence in 10 s ([Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)). Kapwing's 30 s test, however, showed "choppy transitions between shots", a time-of-day shift and a studded leather jacket gradually becoming a bomber jacket ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/is-seedance-2-5-actually-better-than-2-0-heres-what-i-found/)). The evidence supports keeping cuts inside one generation when the shots share location, light and cast and total ≤15 s. Generate per shot for anything longer or when setups differ. Either way, reuse the same reference pack in the same upload order with identical identity wording. Overlapping identity files get averaged into drift, so use one sheet per character with the strongest first ([seedance2video.io](https://seedance2video.io/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-troubleshooting)). Change one variable per retry ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-use-cases/can-seedance-25-keep-the-same-character-across-shots/)).

For continuity and repair, use the locked tasks. **Extension** continues "from the source clip's last frame—subject position, movement direction, lighting", and MOV in and out gives smoother joins. **Time-range editing** ("Edit seconds 4–7 of Video 1. Replace only…") fixes a bad stretch without re-rolling the shot. **First/last frame** suits scene changes ([docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)). Repair in that order: edit the bad range, extend from the last good frame, and re-roll only as a last resort.

## The @viznfr note is right on method, loose on precision

The note's pipeline is shot list, then a Claude-scripted Blender blockout with flat colour proxies and one timed camera move, then upload to Higgsfield Genjutsu with a structured prompt. That is the same technique as the official guide, Curious Refuge and CozyClay. **Genjutsu** is Higgsfield's video-to-video tool (Motion Transfer and Object Swap modes, plus a Lite tier). It accepts 4–30 s source clips and up to 30 reference images ([Higgsfield Genjutsu](https://higgsfield.ai/genjutsu); [Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-genjutsu)). Higgsfield does not name the underlying model. "It's Seedance 2.5" is speculation ([Kavel](https://www.kavel.ai/blog/higgsfield-genjutsu-price-guide)), although Genjutsu Motion Transfer and Higgsfield's Seedance 2.5 page charge an identical 3/7 credits per second, which is weak circumstantial support. The author's site describes a self-styled "AI Creative Director" ([viznfr.com](https://www.viznfr.com/)). The note ends by gating the build script and full prompt behind a DM waitlist for a "PRO pack", so it is a lead magnet. No affiliation with Higgsfield could be confirmed or ruled out.

Translating the Genjutsu steps is mechanical. The shot list and Blender build are unchanged. "Upload to Genjutsu → Motion Transfer" becomes a **Seedance 2.5 reference-to-video** call with the blockout first in `video_urls` and references in `image_urls`. You then set aspect ratio, duration and resolution explicitly, because reference tasks don't inherit them. The three-block prompt gains a one-sentence brief and per-asset "reference only X, do not inherit Y" lines. Going direct gains explicit control of ratio, duration, resolution and audio, up to 1080p, API automation, the vendor's prompting guidance applied word for word, and, on ModelArk, less than half the Higgsfield cost. It loses Genjutsu's one-click presets, chiefly **Object Swap**, which keeps the rest of a real plate untouched. That is the one case where Genjutsu may still be the better tool.

## First test: one shot, three questions, about $50

The first test should answer three questions cheaply. Does Seedance hold *our* camera move? Does grey or colour-coding leak less? Does the model hold cuts inside one generation? Keep realistic human faces out of it so a filter refusal can't confuse the results. A product hero plus a grey capsule figure described only in text is ideal.

| Step | What to do | Settings and pass criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Shot A blockout | One 5 s continuous move: eye-level (1.6 m) 35 mm dolly-in from 8 m to 4 m on a hero product on a table, a capsule figure crossing frame left to right at 2–4 s, a 10-frame opening hold | Workbench Studio, cavity and shadows on, light grey, plain sky world; overlays, outline and stamp off; 1280×720, 24 fps, H.264, exactly 120 frames; check `ffprobe` and three PNG stills |
| 2. Shot A colour variant | Same file, product in one highlight colour versus all proxies colour-coded | Two MP4s, identical camera |
| 3. Shot B blockout | 10 s, three cuts (wide 0–4, medium 4–7, over-the-shoulder 7–10) in the same set | Same render settings; cuts on whole seconds |
| 4. References | One product photo (Image 1), one environment plate without people (Image 2), optional stylised character sheet (Image 3) | Plain grey backgrounds, no small text, no photoreal faces |
| 5. Shot A drafts on fal | 4 variants (grey/colour × with/without the placeholder line) × 3 takes | reference-to-video, 480p, 16:9, duration 5, audio off; pass = camera path, figure screen direction and 2–4 s timing all match |
| 6. Shot B drafts | 3 takes as one 10 s generation versus 3 takes of each shot (4 s, 3 s, 3 s) as separate generations, trimmed blockout per shot | 480p; pass = correct shot count and order, identity held across cuts |
| 7. Finals | Best Shot A and Shot B at 720p (finalize from draft if the endpoint supports it); one 1080p comparison | Compare against the blockout overlaid at 50% opacity in an editor |
| 8. Optional controls | Same Shot A through ModelArk (cost and parity) and through Genjutsu Motion Transfer (wrapper comparison) | Identical inputs and prompt |

Prompt template for Shot A (tags positional, parameters set outside the text):

```text
@Video1: Coarse blockout. Use it only for camera position, camera movement, framing, subject paths and timing. Do not copy its grey materials, flat colours, empty background or mannequin poses. The [colour] box on the table is the product in @Image1. The grey capsule is a person (described below). Treat the 3D figures as placeholders rather than copying their exact poses or body mechanics.
@Image1: Reference only the product's shape, label and materials.
@Image2: Reference only the café interior, its colour palette and its warm morning light. Do not reference any people.

Brief: A cinematic product shot of [product] on a café table as a barista passes behind it, warm documentary style.

0–2 s: The product rests on the table in soft window light; the café is quiet.
2–4 s: A barista in a dark green apron walks from frame left to frame right behind the table, carrying a tray.
4–5 s: The barista has left frame; steam rises from a cup beside the product.

Global constraints: Morning daylight throughout, consistent colour grade, realistic materials. No text, subtitles, logos other than the product label, or background music. Do not generate motion paths, axes, grids or camera guides.
```

The draft budget comes to about 120 s at 480p: Shot A at 12 × 5 s = 60 s, Shot B as one generation at 3 × 10 s = 30 s, and Shot B per shot at 3 takes × (4 + 3 + 3 s) = 30 s. Finals add about 15 s at 720p and 5 s at 1080p. Using our per-output-second estimates with the blockout input billed (fal ≈$0.27/s at 480p, ≈$0.57/s at 720p, ≈$1.16/s at 1080p), the list cost is about **$32 + $9 + $6 ≈ $46 on fal**. ModelArk is about $20–25 (≈$0.124/s at 480p, ≈$0.28/s at 720p). On the Higgsfield app it is about 545 credits (3/7/16 credits per second at 480p/720p/1080p) plus any unknown video-reference surcharge. With CozyClay's 2–3× miss factor, cap the test at **$100 on fal**, or one $49 Higgsfield plan plus a top-up. Score every take against a fixed sheet: camera path, timing at each whole second, screen direction, leakage, identity and cut count.

The test should also settle these open questions. Does reference-to-video support draft-then-finalize on our host, or only image-to-video? How is the blockout input billed: actual seconds, or a minimum tied to output length? Do grey proxies or colour-coded proxies leak less, and does the placeholder line loosen blocking? Does restating the timeline change timing accuracy compared with an asset map only? How often does left/right mirroring occur, and does stating screen direction in text fix it? Does a 30 fps blockout behave any differently from 24 fps? Does the face filter block our stylised character sheets? Does Genjutsu give any measurable gain over direct Seedance 2.5 on identical inputs?

## Conclusion

The research shifts where the work sits. Seedance 2.5's camera control is a property of the reference clip, not the prompt, so precision becomes a Blender problem: real scale, real lenses, eased curves and a clean render. Blender problems are free to iterate, deterministic and scriptable by Claude Code. The prompt's job narrows to labelling what the clip means and fencing off what it must not leak. That also explains the field's two open fights, grey versus colour and cuts in one generation versus per shot. Both are trade-offs between giving the model more signal and giving it more to copy, and a team should settle them with its own A/B tests rather than by authority.

The Genjutsu framing is best read as a wrapper choice, not a technique. Its credit pricing matches Higgsfield's own Seedance 2.5 page, the same workflow runs at about half the price on ModelArk, and the only feature with no direct equivalent is Object Swap on live footage. The biggest unknowns are fidelity under stress and face-filter friction on character references, not the method itself. No one has published a quantitative adherence test, so the overlay comparison and scoring sheet from this first test will be among the more rigorous evidence available.
