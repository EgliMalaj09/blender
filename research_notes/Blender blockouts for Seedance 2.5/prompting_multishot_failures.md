## (a) Prompt structure for a Blender blockout video reference

### Takeaway
Use the four-part structure: asset mapping in upload order, a one-sentence brief, a continuous whole-second timeline, then global constraints. Open with an explicit role line, for example: "Use Video 1 only for shot order, camera positions, framing, subject paths and timing; do not copy the blockout materials or character appearance." Map each blockout figure to a real subject or a reference image. Sources disagree on whether to restate camera moves in text. The official-style guide and CozyClay say not to re-describe what the clip carries. Curious Refuge found that restating shot timings helped. A reasonable reconciliation is to restate the cut points and the timing, but not the camera motion.

### Cited Findings
**Four-part structure and asset mapping**
- The recommended structure is "Asset mapping + one-sentence brief + timeline or shot order + global constraints". Asset mapping goes in upload order and states whether each image, video or audio file controls the subject, movement, setting, style or sound. The brief is "subject + location + event + genre/style + special camera treatment". — [Seedance 2.5 Prompting Guide Part 1 (docs.seedance.tv)](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- "Do not rely on names written inside reference images… bind every input in a list before describing the story." Every asset should answer "Who or what does it belong to? What specifically should be referenced?" If only part of an asset should be used, also state what must not be inherited. Example: "Video 1: Reference only the two characters' action order. Video 2: Reference only the orbiting camera and editing rhythm. Image 4: Reference only the warm golden backlight… do not reference the people." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Official-guide blockout example (coarse blockout plus 9 stage keyframes, 30 s, 16:9): "Use Video 1 only for the complete shot order, camera positions, framing changes, subject paths, and timing. Do not copy the blockout materials or character appearance. Images 2–10 constrain the character design, environment, and color at each corresponding stage." It then maps time ranges to images ("0–3 seconds corresponds to Image 2; 3–5 to Image 3…") and ends with "Preserve Video 1's shot structure exactly, add no new shots, and keep character appearance consistent with each corresponding keyframe." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Fine-blockout re-render example: "Fully re-render Video 1 while preserving its action paths, camera movement, timing, and framing exactly." It then gives a full description of the new environment and subject, and ends with "Do not generate motion paths, axes, or camera guides." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- "A blockout can define shot order, framing, character paths, action, and lighting rhythm. The prompt must state what to inherit and fully describe any subject or setting that lacks a visual reference." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- fal's guide distinguishes a coarse blockout (simple geometry carrying timing, paths, blocking and camera) from a fine blockout (complete structures, re-rendered for materials and style). Its mapping template is "<Blockout Subject A> in @Video 1 corresponds to <Subject A>", and it suggests stating which of motion path, blocking, camera movement, cuts, lighting or sound rhythm to keep from @Video 1. It says "do not use the gray materials or empty background". These come from search snippets; the page was not fetched. — [fal.ai: How To Use Seedance 2.5](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5)
- Atlas Cloud's guide suggests wording such as "do not use its gray plastic material or empty background". Its checklist asks whether the blockout is clearly marked coarse or fine. — [Atlas Cloud: How to Write Seedance 2.5 Prompts](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/how-to-write-seedance-2.5-prompts)
- Curious Refuge's fix for over-literal copying: "Use the reference video as a guide for camera positions and movements. Treat the 3D figures as placeholders rather than copying their exact poses or body mechanics." — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)
- An open-source tool (DisturbedMediaGroup/seedance-reskin) turns a grey blocking animation into a prompt that "locks motion one-to-one and re-skins everything else". It warns that the closer the blockout is to a finished render, the more the model copies its materials instead of your reference sheets. — [GitHub: seedance-reskin](https://github.com/DisturbedMediaGroup/seedance-reskin)

**Tag syntax per platform**
- fal API docs address inputs as @Video1 and @Image1 (no space). fal's learn guide writes "@Video 1" with a space. Tags map to the reference's position in its array, not to its filename. Runware and Magnific docs describe the same 1-indexed @Image1…@Image30, @Video1, @Audio1 scheme. Evolink's docs suggest lowercase tags. — [fal Reference-to-Video API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api); [Runware multi-reference guide](https://runware.ai/docs/models/bytedance-seedance-2-5/guides/multi-reference-production); [Magnific API docs](https://docs.magnific.com/api-reference/video/seedance-2-5-pro/overview); [Evolink docs](https://evolink.ai/docs/en/api-manual/video-series/seedance2.5/seedance-2.5-overview.md)
- The official-style guide uses plain prose ("Video 1", "Image 2") with no @. Curious Refuge, using Magnific's UI, labelled the environment as Image 1 and the character sheets as Images 2 and 3, alongside the animatic as the video reference. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide); [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)
- No source confirmed a "[Video1]" bracket syntax. — (search, no hits)

**Limits relevant to references**
- Up to 30 s output, up to 50 assets per request, images up to 30 (each ≤4K), video up to 10 clips and 30 s total, audio up to 10 clips and 30 s total. "One to eight subjects is most reliable; split crowded images into single-subject views." Five to ten seconds of video works well for subject reference. Keep editing inputs under 20 s. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- fal: up to 10 reference videos, each 1.8–30.2 s and ≤200 MB, combined under 30.2 s. — [fal Reference-to-Video API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api)
- Platform caps differ: Atlas Cloud reportedly caps reference images at 10, while Runware allows 30 images, 10 videos and 10 audio clips. — [Runware](https://runware.ai/docs/models/bytedance-seedance-2-5/guides/multi-reference-production); [Atlas Cloud R2V](https://www.atlascloud.ai/it/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/llms.txt)

**Timeline**
- "Seedance 2.5 can follow whole-second timestamps. Time ranges should be continuous—0–3 seconds, 3–7 seconds, 7–15 seconds—without unexplained gaps." "If a time range contains too little, the model will improvise; if it contains too much, actions may be omitted or the edit may become frantic." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Global constraints are listed as "camera position, lighting, color, ambience, aspect ratio, no subtitles, or no BGM". The preflight list asks: "Are action, sound, and transition triggers timed explicitly?" — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Third-party guides add: avoid beats longer than about 10 s; packing several actions into one beat can mean only the first is completed; a 30 s generation works best with roughly four or five beats; identity, lens, grade and negatives go in a global block rather than being repeated per beat. These come from snippets of seedance2video.io, tapvid and getstarrd guides, which are unverified third-party sources. — [seedance2video.io troubleshooting](https://seedance2video.io/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-troubleshooting); [Tapvid](https://tapvid.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-guide); [Getstarrd](https://www.getstarrd.app/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-guide)

**Restate camera moves or not (conflict)**
- CozyClay: "Prompt only for what the clip does not carry: materials, weather, wardrobe, mood. Do not describe the camera again in words; the clip already did." — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)
- Official-style Part 2 (editing context): "When movement comes from the source, say 'preserve the original action order and timing exactly' instead of describing the action again and risking drift." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- maxfusion and fal guides (snippets) say restating motion in text can conflict with the reference. — [maxfusion.ai](https://maxfusion.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide); [fal.ai](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5)
- Contradicted by Curious Refuge: "Claude describes the individual shot timings. Although the cuts are already visible in the reference video, repeating that information in the prompt can help Seedance follow the sequence." Their Claude-generated prompt included "shot timing, character actions, sound effects, and camera direction". — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)
- The official blockout example does give time ranges, mapped to keyframe images, but it does not re-describe camera moves. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)

### Inferences
- Best-supported template: (1) asset map with a "reference only X; do not inherit Y" line for each asset; (2) a one-sentence brief; (3) a continuous 0–N s timeline that matches the blockout's cut points and says what happens in each shot (subject, action, emotion), but not how the camera moves; (4) global constraints, which should be static properties only (look, grade, ambience, "no motion paths, axes, camera guides, grey materials, text, subtitles, BGM"). This fits both camps: CozyClay and the guide warn against restating motion, while Curious Refuge's gain came from restating timings and cuts.
- If you do restate a camera move, it must match the clip exactly. A mismatch between text and clip is the conflict the guides warn about.
- Use @Video1 or @Image1 (no space, 1-indexed in upload order) on API platforms such as fal, Runware and Magnific. Use "Video 1" prose where the UI does not parse tags. Either way, keep a mapping list at the top.

### Gaps
- No primary ByteDance (BytePlus or Volcengine) page was fetched to confirm the guide text. The docs.seedance.tv attribution is inferred from video paths.
- Higgsfield and Dreamina tag syntax was not confirmed.
- No controlled A/B test was found on restating camera moves versus leaving them out.

## Timing pitfalls (global rules, "already", density)

### Takeaway
The guide's rules are clear: continuous whole-second ranges, explicit triggers for timed events, and density matched to the duration. I found no source that specifically documents timed events in global rules, or words like "already", causing early reveals. Treat those as plausible practitioner heuristics, not documented behaviour.

### Cited Findings
- Content density: too little content leads to improvisation, too much leads to omitted actions or a frantic edit. Use exact time ("At second 5, whip-pan left…") or relative time ("After the lead presses the shutter, freeze the frame for one second"). — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Global constraints are defined as rules that "apply throughout", such as camera position, lighting, colour, ambience and aspect ratio. Implicitly they are not timed events. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Ending rushes: shorten the preceding action and give the final interval more time; define a final composition and a settle time (third-party). — [seedance2video.io](https://seedance2video.io/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-troubleshooting); [mkanime guide](https://mkanime.ai/guides/fix-seedance-2-5-character-camera-ending)

### Inferences
- A reveal or event placed in global constraints has no time anchor, so the model may show it from frame 0. Keep every event in the timeline only. State-describing words such as "already", "has arrived" or "is holding" carry the same risk. Phrase reveals as transitions at a timestamp instead ("at 6 s the door opens and the product becomes visible"). This is consistent with the guide's "timed explicitly" rule but not directly sourced.

### Gaps
- No source documents the "already" early-reveal effect. The caller should label it as anecdotal or heuristic.

## (b) Multi-shot sequences with consistent characters and products

### Takeaway
There are two workable patterns. (1) A single generation of up to 30 s whose blockout contains the cuts. The official-style guide demonstrates this with a 30 s multi-stage blockout and per-stage keyframes, and Curious Refuge got a working 3-shot, 10 s cut sequence. (2) One generation per shot, assembled in an editor, which CozyClay recommends because cuts are unreliable within one generation. Consistency comes from character turnaround sheets, product photos, per-stage keyframes, extension (forward or backward), time-range editing, and first/last-frame chaining for scene changes. One to eight subjects is the reliable range.

### Cited Findings
**Cuts inside one generation vs one per shot (conflict)**
- CozyClay: "A cut between two setups: two references, two generations. Generate per shot and cut in an editor; models do not hold a cut inside one generation reliably." — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)
- Contradicted by the official-style guide's 30 s blockout example with many shot changes ("Preserve Video 1's shot structure exactly, add no new shots"). — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Also contradicted by Curious Refuge: in a 10 s, 480p draft, Seedance "followed our Blender animation closely, giving us a good amount of control over camera angles, cuts, character positions, and the overall sequence of events." The planned shots were an establishing shot, a medium shot and an over-the-shoulder shot. — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)
- Kapwing's hands-on 30 s test found "choppy transitions between shots", setting and time of day changing, and a studded leather jacket gradually becoming more like a bomber jacket. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/is-seedance-2-5-actually-better-than-2-0-heres-what-i-found/)
- Vendor claims say single-pass 30 s improves continuity, because the model "never has to re-derive who the character is". This is marketing. — [BytePlus Lumina](https://ai.byteplus.com/lumina/en/resource/seedance-2-5-video-generator-guide); [Luma: Think in Scenes](https://lumalabs.ai/learning-center/articles/seedance-2-5-how-to-think-in-scenes)

**Consistency references**
- Curious Refuge used character turnaround sheets with multiple angles, neutral lighting, a plain grey background, and minimal props or text. The environment reference was generated without characters, and small text was avoided in reference images. — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)
- "One to eight subjects is most reliable; split crowded images into single-subject views when possible." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- When references overlap or contradict, they get averaged, which causes identity drift. Never add a second identity file for the same character; put the strong identity reference first; repeat a few visible anchors in identical wording (third-party snippets). — [seedance2video.io](https://seedance2video.io/blog/seedance-2-5-prompt-troubleshooting); [oTechWorld](https://otechworld.com/seedance-2-5-prompt-problems-eight-common-failures-and-how-to-fix-them/)
- WaveSpeed: treat consistency as a controlled workflow with an approval ladder, and change one variable at a time. Changing lighting, age, costume, background and distance together makes it hard to tell which change caused identity drift. — [WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-use-cases/can-seedance-25-keep-the-same-character-across-shots/)
- Products: "references improve control, but they do not guarantee exact product reproduction in every frame". Check logos, packaging and proportions across cuts. — [Nexscope ecommerce workflows](https://www.nexscope.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-ecommerce-video-workflows)
- Keyframes: "Upload every key image separately and in sequence… 'Use Images 1 through N in order as keyframes.'" Separate keyframes align more strictly than a storyboard grid. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)

**Extension, editing, first/last frame (Part 2)**
- Editing, first/last-frame generation and extension are locked tasks. For editing, the aspect ratio follows the source and duration stays close (use adaptive ratio and -1 duration). For first/last frame, mark images as first_frame and last_frame; the aspect ratio follows the first frame. For extension, state forward or backward plus the number of seconds; "MOV input and output usually preserve audio-visual continuity better". With multiple videos, start with "Edit Video 1" or "Extend Video 2". — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- Time-range edit template: "Edit seconds 4–7 of Video 1. Replace only… Preserve identity, expression, body movement, background, camera, lighting, edit rhythm, and original sound. Keep everything before second 4 and after second 7 exactly as in the source." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- Extension: "Continue from the source clip's last frame—subject position, movement direction, lighting, focus, and sound—rather than starting a new scene… The extension may have a small volume difference. Using a Seedance 2.5 source clip and MOV for both input and output generally produces a smoother join." Example: "Do not reintroduce the setting or alter the flower's state in the original last frame." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- Seamless transition between two clips: describe the exit of Video 1, the bridge transformation, and the entry viewpoint of Video 2. "'Connect naturally' is usually not specific enough." Add "No black frame, white flash, or visible hard cut." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- Extend duration is 4–30 s or -1 (auto); Edit inherits the source length (Segmind). One API doc notes that the -1 default picked 10 s in testing, doubling the cost. Use first/last frame for scene changes such as a new location or a time jump. If an extend drifts, pull the last good frame and chain from it so drift stops compounding. Sources disagree on whether extend accepts a target end frame. — [Segmind Extend](https://www.segmind.com/models/seedance-2.5-extend); [tryonr: 3 modes](https://tryonr.com/blog/seedance-2-5-video-editing-extend); [Morphic guide](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/seedance-2-5-guide)
- AI Video Sensei recommends extending rather than regenerating for consistency, and targeted editing rather than a full re-roll when a face changes. — [AI Video Sensei](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/seedance-2-5-character-consistency)

### Inferences
- For a Blender pipeline, the evidence supports this approach. Use cuts inside one generation when the shots share a location, lighting and cast, and the total is 15 s or less (best evidence: Curious Refuge's 10 s). Use per-shot generations when setups differ or for longer pieces, with the same reference pack (turnaround sheets and product photos) in the same upload order every time, and identical identity wording.
- Ending a shot on a moving camera to help cut continuity is not documented. The extension guidance ("continue… movement direction") and the transition guidance (exit, bridge, entry with continuous camera direction and speed) suggest that a moving exit gives extension and transition modes more to continue from. Treat this as inference.
- Repair order when a generation is mostly right: time-range edit, then extend from the last good frame, then a full re-roll.

### Gaps
- No controlled test was found comparing in-generation cuts with per-shot generation for identity retention.
- No source specifically tested identity drift at 30 s with Blender blockouts. Only Kapwing's text-based 30 s test, showing jacket drift, exists.
- The optimal number of character references (1 sheet or several angles) was not quantified beyond "1–8 subjects".

## Iteration economics (draft, seed, resolution, upscaling)

### Takeaway
Draft at 480p using the platform's draft-then-finalize linkage, not just a fixed seed. Finalizing reproduces the draft at 1080p, while re-running at 1080p with the same seed produces a different take. Native output tops out at 1080p, and 4K requires an upscaler. Fix the camera in Blender first, because a re-export there costs nothing.

### Cited Findings
- Curious Refuge started at 10 s, 16:9, audio on, 480p draft: "We recommend starting in draft mode before committing to a higher-resolution generation." The Blender starter kit renders 720p reference video, "plenty for guiding an AI video model". — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude)
- Segmind measured that a 480p draft and its finalize are the same video (corr 0.96). A direct 1080p render with the same seed had a mean correlation of 0.257 against the draft, so the match comes from the draft linkage (draft_task_id), not the seed. Only 5 clips were tested. — [Segmind: Draft→Final tested](https://blog.segmind.com/seedance-2-5-draft-final-5-clips-tested-and-the-real-cost-math/)
- On fal's image-to-video endpoint, a draft boolean produces a 480p preview and a draft_id, which can be finalized at 1080p within 7 days. It is unclear whether reference-to-video has the same flag. — [fal Image-to-Video API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/image-to-video/api)
- Krea's finalize regenerates at 1080p from the draft's prompt, references, seed, duration and aspect ratio. Krea corrected an earlier claim of native 4K: the outputs are 480p, 720p and 1080p. — [Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-draft-mode-extend-reframe-guide)
- A draft costs roughly one fifth of a 1080p render (Flick). Upscaling a 480p or 720p render invents detail, while draft-then-render keeps the chosen take at native 1080p. — [Flick](https://flick.art/blog/seedance-2-5-draft-mode)
- Segmind's upscaled and native 1080p outputs diverged even with the same prompt and seed. The upscale billed only $0.069 more than native 720p. — [Segmind upscaled vs native](https://blog.segmind.com/seedance-2-5-upscaled-vs-native-1080p-side-by-side/)
- Prices: BytePlus ModelArk 1080p is about $2.84 per 5 s, so 30 s costs about $17, and "a six-shot sequence can cost two or three times its list price" with misses (CozyClay). fal 480p reference-to-video is about $0.22 per second. — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/); [fal R2V API](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api)
- Seedance.tv (platform) offers only 480p and 720p for 2.5. — [Seedance.tv troubleshooting](https://www.seedance.tv/zh/blog/seedance-2-5-troubleshooting-guide)

### Inferences
- Use finalize for a draft you like rather than re-rolling at 1080p. Upscale only beyond 1080p (for 4K delivery). Set an explicit duration rather than -1 (auto) to avoid surprise costs.

### Gaps
- Draft and finalize support on reference-to-video (video reference) endpoints specifically is not confirmed across platforms.

## (c) Failure modes catalogue with fixes

### Takeaway
The documented failures are: overlay or gizmo leakage; blockout material and colour leakage; stiff, video-game-like motion copied from the blockout figures; left/right mirroring; low subject visibility; late-clip drift in long references or generations; identity and wardrobe drift over 30 s; choppy cuts; and refusal of real-face references. Most fixes are upstream: clean the export, use neutral or flat shading, use short clips, write explicit "placeholder" wording, and add identity references.

### Cited Findings
- **Overlays becoming objects.** "Outlines, gizmos, the ground grid, a selection highlight, a frame counter: any of these can come back in the generation as an object." Fix: a flat clay pass with a plain sky, matching delivery aspect ratio, 3–5 s, nothing drawn on top. — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/). The guide also says: "Remove motion paths, axes, and camera frustums so they do not leak into the result", and adds "Do not generate motion paths, axes, or camera guides" to the prompt. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- **Proxy colours or materials in the output.** "Colour is a cue the model preserves… Grey or flat clay… A single highlight colour on the subject is the most you should add." — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/). Conflicting approach: seedance-reskin uses flat, saturated, distinct colours per object (to disambiguate subjects) and explicitly tells the model not to copy placeholder materials. It also warns that a near-final-looking blockout gets copied. — [seedance-reskin](https://github.com/DisturbedMediaGroup/seedance-reskin). Prompt fix: "Do not copy the blockout materials or character appearance" ([docs.seedance.tv](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)) or "do not use the gray materials or empty background" ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5)). If grey still leaks, map each placeholder to a final asset and ask for a full re-render (snippet synthesis of fal and Atlas guides).
- **Lighting of the white model.** Flat grey with a basic shadow reads better to the model than a pitch-black scene (Porto Theme, third-party). — [Porto Theme](https://www.portotheme.com/from-blender-blockout-to-final-shot-how-seedance-2-5s-3d-white-model-previz-changes-3d-workflows/)
- **Video-game-like or stiff motion copied from the figures.** "Seedance can follow the Blender reference almost too closely, including its unnatural character movements." The blockout had unnatural wrists, awkward twisting and sliding characters. Fixes: iterate the animatic with Claude, and use the prompt "Treat the 3D figures as placeholders rather than copying their exact poses or body mechanics." — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude). CozyClay: "Keep the figure grey so it invents the person instead of preserving a mannequin." — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)
- **Object interactions unconvincing.** Curious Refuge reported the bottle knock-over "wasn't entirely convincing". — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude). ByteDance acknowledges that complex motion and multi-subject interaction still leave room for improvement, as cited by Nexscope. — [Nexscope](https://www.nexscope.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-ecommerce-video-workflows)
- **Left/right mirroring.** TKVRPG (note.com, Sep 2026, Claude, Blender, Higgsfield, Seedance 2.5) found the camera trajectory accurately reproduced but mirrored in one case: "even if you provide a pre-viz, 'left and right' are not guaranteed". Same creator: the subject was visible for only 1 s of 5 s, and one shot took 8 remakes. No documented fix. — [TKVRPG note.com](https://note.com/tkvrpg/n/nfa791c421336?hl=en)
- **Camera drift late in clips.** "Longer references cost more to generate against and drift more toward the end of the clip." Keep references 3–5 s and matched to the shot. — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/). Atlas Cloud (vendor): without structural guidance, long generation windows cause spatial coordinates to drift across the timeline. — [Atlas Cloud camera control guide](https://www.atlascloud.ai/it/blog/guides/seedance-2.5-camera-control-guide)
- **Camera-prompt unreliability (motivation for blockouts).** Camera language works "as a tendency rather than a specification. The same prompt produces a different angle and distance on each run." — [CozyClay](https://cozyclay.org/seedance-camera-control/)
- **Identity and wardrobe drift over 30 s, choppy transitions, time-of-day shifts.** Kapwing's test showed jacket drift, choppy transitions, and setting and time-of-day changes. The reviewer notes that the time of day was not specified. Fixes: specify the time of day and lighting in global constraints, use identity references, edit or extend rather than re-roll. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/is-seedance-2-5-actually-better-than-2-0-heres-what-i-found/); [AI Video Sensei](https://aivideosensei.com/guides/seedance-2-5-character-consistency)
- **Extend drift.** Re-chain from the last good frame; change one variable per retry. — [tryonr](https://tryonr.com/blog/seedance-2-5-video-editing-extend)
- **Text in references.** Avoid small text in reference images (Curious Refuge). Text in the scene is usually generated content rather than a watermark (Seedance.tv). Storyboards should "avoid oversharpening, clutter, and large blocks of text". — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-control-ai-videos-with-blender-and-claude); [Seedance.tv troubleshooting](https://www.seedance.tv/zh/blog/seedance-2-5-troubleshooting-guide); [docs.seedance.tv](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- **Realistic-face reference refusals.** Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 cannot directly use a realistic real-person face as a first frame or reference. The sanctioned route is liveness or real-person verification and a verified asset group. Adobe Firefly users report AI-generated faces being classified as real and blocked, while the same inputs work on other platforms. Firefly guidance says not to blur or stylize a real face to evade detection, and the BytePlus acceptable use policy bars circumventing safety filters. — [APIYI face asset FAQ](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/faq/seedance2-asset-face-reference); [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/questions-404/seedance-2-5-firefly-is-having-issues-with-ai-generated-faces-it-categorises-ai-generated-faces-as-real-human-face-and-blocks-the-generation-1643856); [Seedance.tv: Firefly real faces blocked](https://www.seedance.tv/blog/seedance-firefly-real-faces-blocked)
- **Overclaims to discount.** One promotional post claims "One-shot results with 100% camera & framing control". The creator reports above contradict that. A JSFILMZ X post promotes the Higgsfield MCP Blender workflow to "stop wasting hundreds of AI credits". — [JSFILMZ on X](https://x.com/JSFILMZ0412/status/2093663876692754713). There is no Blender-specific Seedance release from ByteDance; "Blender + Seedance" is a creator workflow name. — [Toonkit](https://toonkit.io/en/models/seedance-blender)

### Inferences
- Pre-export checklist from the above: Workbench or flat clay render; plain sky or world; overlays, grid, gizmos, paths, frustums, selection outlines and frame counters all off; delivery aspect ratio; 3–5 s per shot clip (longer only when the cuts are deliberate); a figure moving only where motion matters; at most one highlight colour; plausible human-scale proxies. For scale, the CozyClay workflow says "Boxes and a stand-in figure, at real scale."
- Mirroring: check handedness at 480p draft before finalizing, and state screen direction in text ("character A on frame left moving right"). The text fix is inference, not tested.
- Too many proxies: the "1–8 subjects" guidance suggests keeping named or mapped proxies to 8 or fewer and leaving background geometry unmapped and described as environment. This is inference.

### Gaps
- Scale confusion and "too many proxies" have no direct source beyond "at real scale" and "1–8 subjects".
- No Reddit or Discord threads were found. Most community evidence comes from three to four creators. Many vendor sources were read only as search snippets.
- No source tested whether stating "figures are placeholders" degrades blocking accuracy, which is the trade-off of loosening adherence.
