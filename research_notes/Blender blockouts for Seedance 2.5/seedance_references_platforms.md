## How Seedance 2.5 interprets video/blockout references vs first frame, keyframes and storyboard

### Takeaway
Seedance 2.5 splits tasks into two kinds. In a **locked** task (editing, first/last frame, extension), the input fixes the output's timeline or aspect ratio. In an **unlocked/reference** task (ordinary references, storyboards, keyframes, blockouts), the inputs act as constraints and duration and aspect ratio stay free. A Blender blockout clip is a *reference* (generation) input, not an editing master. Treat it as either a **coarse blockout** (geometry carries paths, staging, camera and timing; map each primitive to a final subject) or a **fine blockout** (structure is already complete; re-render materials, look and setting while keeping action, camera and timing exactly). In the prompt, state explicitly what to inherit and what not to copy (grey materials, empty background, gizmos).

### Cited Findings
- **Locked vs reference:** "Locked tasks include editing, first/last-frame generation, and extension. The input occupies the output timeline or determines its aspect ratio… Unlocked tasks include ordinary references, storyboards, and keyframes. Assets provide semantic or visual constraints while output duration and aspect ratio remain independent. Separate keyframe images usually align more strictly than a storyboard grid." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Locked-task parameter rules (Part 2): video editing means the aspect ratio follows the source and the duration stays close to the original ("Use adaptive ratio and -1 duration"). First/last frame means the aspect ratio follows the first frame, the last frame should match it, and duration can be set independently. Extension means the aspect ratio follows the source; state the direction and seconds, and use MOV input/output for a smoother join. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- Editing can return a clip up to about 0.3 s longer or shorter than the source because of input-frame processing. — sd25-pe skill (local file)
- "A blockout video is a generation reference; it does not automatically become an editing master merely because it is a video." Re-rendering a blockout counts as video generation, not editing. — sd25-pe skill (local file)
- **Blockout guidance:** "A blockout can define shot order, framing, character paths, action, and lighting rhythm. The prompt must state what to inherit and fully describe any subject or setting that lacks a visual reference." Input table: "Blockout: Prefer simple geometry. Remove motion paths, axes, and camera frustums so they do not leak into the result." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- **Coarse blockout example** (30 s, 16:9, one blockout video plus 9 stage keyframe images): "Use Video 1 only for the complete shot order, camera positions, framing changes, subject paths, and timing. Do not copy the blockout materials or character appearance. Images 2–10 constrain the character design, environment, and color at each corresponding stage… 0–3 seconds corresponds to Image 2; 3–5 to Image 3… Preserve Video 1's shot structure exactly, add no new shots." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- **Fine blockout example:** "Fully re-render Video 1 while preserving its action paths, camera movement, timing, and framing exactly. Replace the environment with… Do not generate motion paths, axes, or camera guides." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Coarse vs fine definitions: in a coarse blockout, simple geometry mainly carries action paths, blocking, entrances and exits, camera position and movement, cuts, lighting changes, sound rhythm and spatial relationships, and you "map every geometric object separately to its final subject or prop" (for example, "<Blockout Subject A> in @Video1 corresponds to <Subject A>"). In a fine blockout, character, prop and scene structure is already complete and you mainly change appearance, materials, colours, scene or style while preserving structure, action, space and camera. For a fine blockout, write "Do not use its original gray materials, empty background, or production markers." — sd25-pe skill (local file)
- **Storyboard:** a grid "is useful for defining shot count, framing, and narrative order, but it leaves room for interpretation… Use separate keyframe images when the composition must align more strictly." Keep it to fewer than 15 panels, drawn as line art or stick figures. — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- **Keyframes:** "Upload every key image separately and in sequence. Begin the prompt by stating, 'Use Images 1 through N in order as keyframes.'" — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide). The skill adds that keyframes "control stage order and visible states; they do not promise frame-by-frame reproduction or require a static hold." Use the exact sentence "@ImageN is the first frame." / "@ImageN is the last frame." — sd25-pe skill (local file)
- If a reference video already defines the action and camera accurately, state only which dimensions to inherit; restating the action can conflict with the reference. By default a generation task does not inherit the video's identity, clothing or scene. — sd25-pe skill (local file); Part 2 says the same thing: say "preserve the original action order and timing exactly" instead of re-describing the action — [docs.seedance.tv Part 2](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-video-editing-guide)
- Do not write aspect ratio, duration, resolution, fps or the audio toggle into the prompt; set them as API/UI parameters. — sd25-pe skill (local file)
- Prompt structure: asset mapping, then a one-sentence brief, then a timeline or shot order, then global constraints. Use whole-second, continuous time ranges (0–3, 3–7…). — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Vendor practice advice: export the blockout flat (clay/workbench), at the delivery aspect ratio, 3–5 s per shot, with no outlines, gizmos, grid, selection highlight or frame counter, because "any of these can come back in the generation as an object." Keep the figure grey "so it invents the person instead of preserving a mannequin." Generate one shot per setup and cut in an editor; "models do not hold a cut inside one generation reliably." Do not describe the camera again in words. Use a first frame for locked-off shots and a reference clip for camera moves. — [cozyclay.org](https://cozyclay.org) (vendor blog, Sept 2026; its "don't re-describe camera" and "cuts unreliable" advice is opinion, and the official guide's 30 s multi-shot blockout example contradicts the cut claim)
- ByteDance's own examples reportedly include clay and white-model references (cozyclay). Third-party summaries say white-model references "set poses, camera angles and motion paths" and that the model then builds lighting itself. — [cozyclay.org](https://cozyclay.org); [forvideo.ai](https://forvideo.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-reference-guide) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- For a Blender test, the cleanest official-style pattern is: `@Video1` = blockout (state "coarse" or "fine" and list exactly what to inherit), `@Image1..N` = character, set and style refs, plus an explicit primitive-to-subject mapping and "do not use grey materials, empty background, axes, motion paths, camera frustums." Leave the camera out of the text, or describe it only lightly.
- Because a blockout is an unlocked reference, you must set duration and aspect ratio yourself. Match them to the blockout clip, since nothing forces them.

### Gaps
- No ByteDance-hosted page was reachable to confirm the guide is official. docs.seedance.tv is a third-party re-host.
- No official quantitative statement on how tightly a coarse blockout's timing is followed (frame accuracy). The skill says time ranges "are event budgets, not frame-accurate edit points."

## Exact input limits and output options

### Takeaway
The commonly agreed hard limits are ≤30 images (each ≤4K), ≤10 videos totalling ≤30 s, ≤10 audio clips totalling ≤30 s, and ≤50 assets overall. fal adds per-video limits of 1.8–30.2 s and ≤200 MB, in MP4/MOV. Output is 4–30 s at about 24 fps (unconfirmed by ByteDance). Output resolution is the most conflicted spec: 480p and 720p are available everywhere, 1080p is on fal, Magnific, Higgsfield and (per some reports) ModelArk, and 4K is enterprise-only or marketing. 4:3 is supported per most API listings.

### Cited Findings
- Up to 50 assets per request. Images: up to 30, each ≤4K. Video: up to 10 clips, 30 s total. Audio: up to 10 clips, 30 s total. Clips up to 30 s. "Five to ten seconds works well for subject reference; keep editing inputs under 20 seconds when possible." — [docs.seedance.tv Part 1](https://docs.seedance.tv/en/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide); the skill restates these as *hard* limits and treats 1–8 image subjects and 5–10 s clips as recommendations only — sd25-pe skill (local file)
- fal: video refs are MP4 or MOV, up to 10, "each video must be 1.8 to 30.2 seconds and no larger than 200 MB; combined duration must not exceed 30.2 seconds"; 30 images, 10 videos, 10 audio clips, 50 total; at least one reference image or video required — [fal API docs](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api) (search snippet)
- **Conflict:** a GitHub issue in a third-party tool claims fal's Seedance 2.0/2.0 Fast/2.5 take "video_urls, up to 3, 2–15 s combined" — [ArkeStudio issue #1119](https://github.com/michaeljosiah/ArkeStudio/issues/1119) (title only). This probably reflects Seedance 2.0's older limits. fal's own docs (10 videos / 30.2 s) are more authoritative for 2.5. Novoads separately reports a cap of 9 images, 3 videos and 3 audio files on Higgsfield, which contradicts Higgsfield's own docs (30/10/10) — [novoads](https://novoads.ai/en/blog/higgsfield-vs-seedance); [Higgsfield API docs](https://docs.higgsfield.ai/docs/models/seedance-2-5/reference-to-video) (search snippet)
- Reference-video technical constraints from one reseller: each clip 2–30 s, **24–60 fps**, **≥409,600 pixels** (about 640×640; for example 854×480 = 409,920 just passes, while 640×360 fails), 30 s total — [hiapi.ai](https://www.hiapi.ai/en/models/seedance-2.5-reference-to-video) (search snippet; reseller, probably mirroring ModelArk limits)
- Higgsfield API: media must be public URLs (asset:// rejected) — [Higgsfield API docs](https://docs.higgsfield.ai/docs/models/seedance-2-5/reference-to-video) (search snippet)
- **Output duration:** 4–30 s per most sources, including Higgsfield's help centre and OpenRouter. Scenario offers "Auto" to match the reference clip length — [Higgsfield help](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-use-seedance); [Scenario](https://www.scenario.com/blog/seedance-2-5-4k-ai-video-generator-scenario) (search snippet)
- **Aspect ratios:** 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, 9:16, 21:9, plus adaptive/auto. Some providers omit 21:9, and one lists 9:21. 21:9 pixel sizes are quoted as 992×432 at 480p and 1470×630 at 720p — [GitHub awesome-seedance-2.5-api-prompts](https://github.com/Anil-matcha/awesome-seedance-2.5-api-prompts); [apixo](https://apixo.ai/docs/models/video/seedance-2-5) (search snippet). **4:3 is supported** per these listings.
- **fps:** "H.264 High profile MP4 at a true constant 24 fps… stereo AAC audio at 32 kHz" (third-party test). Flixly says "no fps figure appears in any ByteDance source for 2.5, and the API exposes no frame-rate parameter." HiAPI says `fps` and `seed` are not accepted — [flixly](https://www.flixly.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-explained-2026); [hiapi guide](https://www.hiapi.ai/en/blog/seedance-2-5-reference-to-video-api-guide) (search snippet). Token formulas everywhere use ×24, which is consistent with 24 fps.
- **Resolution conflict:**
  - fal schema: `resolution` = 480p / 720p (default) / 1080p — [fal API docs](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api) (search snippet)
  - OpenRouter review (3 Sep 2026): no 1080p or 4K listed (720p cap) — [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/seedance-2-5-review/) (search snippet)
  - ModelArk: some say only 480p/720p are listed; others quote 1080p at $11.70/M tokens (about $0.57/s) — [orcarouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-explained); [cellcog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-pricing/) (search snippet)
  - 4K with 10-bit colour plus a 480p "Draft mode" is "for selected enterprises only. Higgsfield and OpenArt advertise 480p/720p/1080p/4K — [Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-on-higgsfield-2026); [OpenArt](https://openart.ai/ai-model/seedance-2-5/) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Export the Blender blockout at ≥854×480 (safer at 1280×720), 24 fps, as MP4 H.264, at the target aspect ratio, and ≤30 s. A 12 s blockout fits on all platforms.
- Draft at 480p and finish at 720p. 1080p is available on fal, Magnific and Higgsfield; do not plan on 4K.

### Gaps
- No official ByteDance spec sheet was reachable, so resolution tiers, fps and the minimum input pixel count are not confirmed first-hand.
- Exact fal field names beyond `image_urls`/`video_urls`/`audio_urls`, `resolution` and `task`, such as `duration` values and `generate_audio`, were not retrieved. Check the fal `/api` page or the OpenAPI JSON.
- The max input video resolution and file size for ModelArk were not found.

## Platforms, video-reference support, pricing, regions, face filters

### Takeaway
Every API host checked (BytePlus ModelArk, fal, Higgsfield API, Replicate, Atlas Cloud, plus the Magnific, Higgsfield and Dreamina UIs) exposes a reference-to-video mode with video refs. BytePlus ModelArk and Replicate are the cheapest per second, at about $0.10/s for 480p and $0.23/s for 720p. fal is about 2× that. Reference video *input* is billed as extra seconds at a 0.6× token rate (ModelArk, fal, Higgsfield API). Realistic human faces in reference images or videos are blocked on the official API path.

### Cited Findings
**Prompt tagging / API surface**
- fal endpoint `bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video` takes `image_urls`, `video_urls`, `audio_urls`, which you address by position in the prompt as **@Image1 / @Video1 / @Audio1** — [fal API docs](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/api); [fal learn](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/what-is-seedance-2-5) (search snippet). fal also has a US-hosted variant, `bytedance/seedance-2.5/us/reference-to-video` — [fal US](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/us/reference-to-video)
- The official-style guide uses plain "Video 1 / Image 2" wording (docs.seedance.tv). The skill uses compact `@Video1` with no space — sd25-pe skill (local file). I found no `[Video1]` bracket syntax in any source.
- BytePlus ModelArk model ID: `dreamina-seedance-2-5-260628`, billed per token — [cometapi](https://www.cometapi.com/seedance-2-5-api-pricing/); [cellcog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-pricing/) (search snippet)

**Token billing formula (ModelArk-style, also used by fal and Higgsfield API)**
- tokens ≈ (input video duration + output duration) × out_width × out_height × 24 / 1024. Image and audio refs do not count as video input. With video input, the rate is multiplied by 0.6 — [Higgsfield API playground](https://open.higgsfield.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/reference-to-video/playground); [apiyi docs](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/api-capabilities/seedance2/overview) (search snippet)
- **Conflict on the minimum billable input:** one source says billable input = max(round(input), a minimum tied to output length), for example 3 s for a 4 s output and 7 s for a 10 s output. Another says max(input, output duration) — [sjolt](https://sjolt.ai/pricing/seedance-2-5-video-input) (search snippet). Actual billing uses `usage.completion_tokens`.
- Only successful generations are charged (BytePlus, quoted) — [cellcog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-pricing/) (search snippet)

**Regions**
- Dreamina launched Seedance 2.5 globally on 31 Jul 2026 but not initially in the US. Dreamina later said it is live for US users, though rollout varies by account. **Face/likeness filter**
- ModelArk/BytePlus docs (quoted secondhand) say Seedance 2.5/2.0 "do not support direct uploads of reference images or videos containing real human faces." Some resellers advertise "uncensored" variants (Wiro); treat those with caution.
